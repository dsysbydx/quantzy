Multi-Asset Trend Book

17 ETFs plus T-bills, rebalanced daily by mean-variance, with up to 1.3× gross exposure. Expected return is a class Sharpe prior (for Treasuries, half way to the yield curve's carry) plus a small slow-trend tilt (price against its 200-day EMA and the 64/256 EMA crossover) and, on the equity ETFs, the variance risk premium (VIX² minus SPY's realized variance). On top run three rule-based overlays: 0.5× NAV in TLT over the last two trading days of each month, when bond index funds extend duration; over the same two days, up to 0.25× NAV long or short SPY against the month's stock–bond rebalancing by balanced funds (short when stocks have beaten bonds month to date); and, from 2011, 0.05× NAV in VIXY (VIX futures) while the VIX is calm — on once it closes at or below 13, sold once it reaches 30. Daily returns are net of per-ETF trading cost (1 to 5 bp one way) and of borrowing at the T-bill rate plus 0.5%. Sharpe is return over vol, with no cash deduction.

mu_i = vol_i × (SR_class + 0.05 × trend_ema200_i + 0.037 × ewmac64_256_i + 0.091 × vrp [equity ETFs] + 0.10 × dollar value [UUP]) SR = equity 0.30 · GLD 0.20 · DBC 0.15 · UUP 0 · IEF/TLT 0.5 × 0.25 + 0.5 × (long yield − 3m bill) / vol (7%, 14%) risk = ewma_blend(5, 20, 60) sizing: risk aversion 2, no hard vol limit (~10% vol) limits = long only · max 1.3× gross, borrowed vs T-bills · 20% per ETF as a share of the invested book trading = optimizer band 5 bp × 48 on every leg incl. cash · charged 1–5 bp by ETF, cash free · borrow at T-bill + 50 bp period = train / test split

12Caveats

  • The Treasury prior moves half way to the yield curve's carry (long yield minus the 3-month bill, over long-run vol). The term spread has forecast Treasury excess returns since 1962. In this backtest it mainly cut bonds during the 2022–24 inversion (from 18% to 8% of capital) and lifted test-window Sharpe from 1.07 to 1.14; before 2017 it changed little.
  • The dollar value tilt leans toward UUP when DXY is below its 5-year average and away when it is above, because currencies drift back toward purchasing-power parity over years. It was the one value measure that worked here (252-day IC +0.43); equity value (CAPE, regional and sector reversal) lowered Sharpe at the book's horizon and is not used. UUP is a small part of the book, so it adds about 0.02 Sharpe.
  • The Treasury month-end overlay is a separate rule-based position, nearly uncorrelated with the core (+0.05). It has persisted since 1977 (t 5.1 on a 30-year par bond) but has weakened by period, as published anomalies tend to: its Sharpe on excess return was 1.54 in 2002–08, 0.82 in 2008–17 and 0.43 in 2017–26. It is sized at 0.5×, half of what its recent Sharpe would justify, which keeps gross exposure under about 1.8× on those days. The charts of weights and positioning show the core only.
  • The pension rebalancing overlay trades against balanced and pension funds' month-end rebalancing (Harvey, Mazzoleni and Melone, 2025): over the last two trading days it holds −0.25 × clip(z / 2, −1, 1) of SPY, where z is SPY minus IEF return month to date, through the close before entry, over its expected spread. It added 0.03 Sharpe (1.34 → 1.37, both halves) and is nearly uncorrelated with the book (+0.04), but it is lumpy — the best five months gave 58% of its gain — and fading since publication (overlay Sharpe 0.91, 0.77, 0.35 by period), so it runs at half the tested size. It shorts SPY in about 61% of months.
  • The VIX hedge overlay is insurance chosen deliberately, not an alpha that passed the research bar. It is −0.4 correlated with the book, cut the 2020 loss from −8.4% to −5.7%, and costs about 0.4% of NAV a year on average (−5.7% in calm 2017). It did nothing in 2022, the book's worst drawdown, so it lowers Calmar slightly. Its thresholds were the best of a nine-cell grid; expect less than the backtest shows.
  • The VRP alpha adds a market-wide equity view: overweight equities when implied variance is high relative to realized. It lifted Sharpe from 1.06 to 1.09 (before the Treasury carry change) and cut the worst drawdown from −18.2% to −16.0%, but about three quarters of its gain came in 2008 and 2021, and before 2008 its evidence on SPY is weak (t 1.1). It does not speed up re-entry after a V-shaped crash: at the March 2020 low it was at its most bearish.
  • The trend budget is split between EMA-200 and the EWMAC 64/256 crossover. The crossover forecasts next-day returns better (IC +0.018 against +0.006) but adds no Sharpe in this book. The swap was made on the signal evidence and cut turnover by 14%. Slower trend exits later in a sudden crash: September to October 2008 lost about 3 points more than with EMA-200 alone.
  • The slow crossover needs history until September 2008, so the backtest starts there and misses May to August 2008, when the EMA-200-only book lost 10.7%.
  • About 40 configurations were tried before choosing this one. The trend gain held in both the train and test windows and on two risk-model variants, but the exact settings still carry some selection bias.
  • The class Sharpe priors are judgment calls. The book holds roughly a third in Treasuries, which helped from 2008 to 2020 and hurt in 2022.
  • Risk aversion 2 is a portfolio-level setting. It runs at about 10% vol with today's alphas, but a fixed risk aversion lets vol drift: 9.2% in training, 10.3% in the test window, inside the 8–12% band. Adding alphas will raise it further.
  • Reaching 10% means lowering risk aversion from 3 to 1.5, which concentrates the book. In the earlier EMA-200-only book from May 2008, the diversified λ = 3 version had a Sharpe of 1.13 at 6.7% vol, against 0.99 at λ = 1.5.
  • Borrowing is charged at T-bill + 0.5%, which is close to futures financing. A retail margin loan often costs 1 to 1.5% over T-bills, which would take about 0.3% a year off the return.
  • Trades are charged an estimated 1 bp (QQQ, GLD, IEF, TLT), 2 bp (sector SPDRs, EFA, EEM) or 5 bp (DBC, UUP) one way, with no market-impact model; check against real fills. The optimizer separately uses a much larger 5 bp × 48 penalty as a no-trade band: trading closer to the daily target lowered Sharpe at every looser setting tested. T-bill yield comes from ^IRX.
